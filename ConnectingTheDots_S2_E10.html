What Decides Leadership?

Last week’s question was who prepared the ranking. This week’s question: does arriving first decide leadership?

Onward — edition 10.

OpenAI (ChatGPT) got there first. Google (Bard, now Gemini) followed, and Anthropic (Claude) opened its public site a few months after that.

Menlo, an Anthropic investor, shows the head start did not last. OpenAI held half of enterprise spend on AI models in 2023. By late 2025, Anthropic led.

Coding was the wedge. Menlo had Anthropic’s coding share rising through 2025 while OpenAI’s stood relatively still. Even that edge is being tested: among the US businesses Ramp tracks, most with a coding agent use Claude Code, yet Codex is growing fastest month to month.

The shift followed a pattern. In 2025, spend moved first. In 2026, business adoption and public attention followed together.

Leadership slips. RIM’s BlackBerry, the executive’s phone, was about one in five smartphones shipped worldwide in 2009. By the third quarter of 2010, Apple had passed it.¹

Arriving first gives you a head start. The leadership remains with whoever does the job better. Attention is still catching up.

¹ To be fair, the keypad and the security were real strengths, and some people still miss them. What changed was the job: touchscreens and apps.
