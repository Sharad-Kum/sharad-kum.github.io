Attention Allocation Systems

Most content surfaced in a feed traces back to one of two sources — someone in the network engaged with it, or the platform recommended or promoted it.

Relationships establish the network. Algorithms increasingly allocate the attention.

The Network Layer
Relationships built deliberately, over years, with 1st-degree connections.
→
The Allocation Layer
Algorithms deciding, post by post, which of those relationships get to see what.

Distribution seems to slow considerably after the first 2 to 3 hours. During that active window, impressions often continue accumulating much faster than unique reach — repeated exposure within a small audience, or something else entirely. Even after several days, many posts appear to reach only a small proportion of one's own 1st-degree connections.

If professional networks are gradually evolving into systems where relationships establish the network but algorithms allocate the attention, perhaps the discussion should no longer center on reach or discoverability, but on how opportunity itself is allocated before an idea is distributed, discovered, and ultimately evaluated.
