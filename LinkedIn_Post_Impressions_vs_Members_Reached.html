Impressions vs Members Reached

LinkedIn defines Impressions as the number of times a post is shown, and Members Reached as the number of distinct members who saw it, excluding repeat displays.

If a post is shown to a member, and Members Reached represents distinct members who saw it — how should a significant, persistent gap between the two be interpreted?

Four questions beneath the metric
Metrics & Interpretation
Are these measuring different stages of visibility, attention, or content consumption?
Visibility & Distribution
What determines the audience a post is first exposed to, and how far it travels over time?
Influence & Discoverability
How much do content, account attributes, network relationships, and subscription tiers shape what surfaces, and to whom?

Could some content remain outside a member's field of view despite being valuable to them? When content is repeatedly surfaced to the same audience, where is the balance between discoverability, relevance, and fatigue?

This feels less like a question about analytics and more like a question about how professional networks determine what we see, what we do not see, and which ideas get the opportunity to travel. I suspect this note is the beginning of the inquiry rather than its conclusion.
