When an Idea Doesn’t Reach Its Own Network

Continuing the inquiry on Analytics, Content Visibility & How Ideas Travel on LinkedIn — one thought has only become stronger: perhaps the right questions were never asked, or perhaps the answers simply don't matter to the system.

At what point does a professional network stop behaving like a network and start behaving like a recommendation surface? One is built on relationships. The other, on prediction.

A Network
Built on relationships — connections chosen and built over years.
→
A Recommendation Surface
Built on prediction — exposure determined by a system, not a relationship.

If a professional conversation reaches only a tiny fraction of one's own Level-1 network, and very little beyond it, what conclusion should be drawn? That the idea lacked relevance — or that it never traveled far enough for its relevance to even be tested?

Recommendation systems exist for good reasons — scale demands them, noise demands them, relevance demands them. But should ideas become progressively harder to discover, even within networks consciously built over years? Perhaps the more fundamental question is this: what determines how far an idea travels — the idea, or the system?
